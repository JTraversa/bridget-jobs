# Job Search Master List

A ranked list of where to look, built around this profile: BS Psychology, master's finishing fall 2026, ~5 years experience across three research modes (academic behavioral/experimental lab management at UMD Smith, clinical regulatory ops, industry UX research at Meta). Based in Reston VA, open to remote or relocation.

**Ranking principle:** fit density per hour spent. Tier 1 places have the highest ratio of "roles she could actually get" to "listings she has to read." Tier 3 places are real but thin, so they get checked monthly, not weekly.

**The one thing that makes this profile unusual:** three distinct research modes in five years. Most people at this stage have one. It means she can credibly apply into three different lanes, which is why the list below is grouped by lane and not by board.

## Tier 2: check every two weeks

### 5. AAPOR Career Center, plus DC-AAPOR

`aapor.org/archives/aapor-career-center/` and separately `dc-aapor.org/job-postings/browse-job-postings/`. AAPOR is the professional association for survey and public opinion research, roughly 2,000 members across academia, government, nonprofits and industry. Low volume, very high relevance. **The DC chapter maintains its own local board**, which is unusually well targeted for someone in Reston and which almost nobody outside the field knows exists.

### 6. HFES Job Board

`jobs.hfes.org`. Human Factors and Ergonomics Society. Worth checking specifically because **Human Factors is one of her top endorsed skills**, carried over from Meta. This board is where human factors roles concentrate, and the applicant pools are small.

### 7. Market research and consumer insights

Her Smith School work is consumer behavior, which is a direct credential here.
- **GreenBook Insights Careers**: `greenbook.org/insights/insights-careers`
- **Quirk's**: `quirks.com` (free job postings)
- **Insights Association**: trade body for ~30,000 US insights professionals
- **Insights Career Network**: `insightscareernetwork.org`

### 8. Higher ed, if staying in academia is acceptable

Only worth it for a better institution, better pay, or a title bump. Same work, known quantity.
- **HERC**: `hercjobs.org`, free, syndicates directly from member institutions' HR systems
- **HigherEdJobs**: `higheredjobs.com`, largest volume

### 9. Federal and federal-adjacent

- **USAJOBS**: `usajobs.gov`
- **Census Bureau**, **BLS**, **NCES**: the largest survey research employers in the country, and a natural fit for a behavioral or survey background
- **Office of Evaluation Sciences (GSA)**: `oes.gsa.gov`, the federal applied behavioral science team in DC. This is her current job with a policy target instead of a business school one. Small team, posts rarely, worth a standing alert

### 10. Behavioral science shops, direct

Small, so they post infrequently and never syndicate. Check the careers page directly.
- **Behavioural Insights Team (BIT) North America**: `bi.team/careers`
- **ideas42**: `ideas42.org/careers`
- **Irrational Labs**: `irrationallabs.com`
- **Center for Advanced Hindsight / Common Cents Lab** (Duke)

## Tier 3: monthly, or situational

### 11. Research tooling companies

Companies that sell research infrastructure hire research coordinators and ResearchOps people by those exact titles, and most are remote-first.
`userinterviews.com` · `usertesting.com` · `dscout.com` · `prolific.com` · `qualtrics.com` · `sago.com`

**Prolific is worth a specific look**: it runs academic participant panels, which is her exact world commercialized.

### 12. Nonprofit and mission research

**Idealist** (`idealist.org`) is the main board. Also PATH, FHI 360, Population Council if global health interests her.

### 13. The clinical regulatory return path

Only if she wants it. Her year at TRI doing eTMF and document review is a real credential that does not expire quickly, and this lane pays well.
**ACRP** (`careers.acrpnet.org`) · **CenterWatch** · **PharmiWeb.jobs** · **BioSpace**
Target employers: Emmes (Rockville), Technical Resources International (Bethesda, her former employer, warm re-entry), and the NIH support contractors.

### 14. Tech-specific aggregators

Only if the UXR lane becomes the primary focus. **Built In**, **Wellfound**, `uiuxjobsboard.com`.

### 15. Decision science community channels

No formal job board, but jobs circulate through the **SJDM quarterly newsletter** (`sjdm.org/newsletters/`), the **jdm-society mailing list** (open to read and post), and **Decision Science News** (`decisionsciencenews.com/?cat=9`). Low volume, but these are the roles nobody else sees.

### 16. ResearchOps Community

Slack-based community, founded 2018, practitioners in 100+ countries. Not a job board so much as where ReOps roles get mentioned before they are posted. Worth joining for the network more than the listings.

### 17. UMD internal and alumni channels

She is current staff and a UMBC alum. University internal transfer boards, Smith School career services, and Handshake alumni access are all cheap to check and occasionally surface things that never go public.

## Worth deliberately skipping

- **ORISE / Zintellect** federal research fellowships. These are stipend appointments with no benefits progression, aimed at people with no work history. At five years plus a master's, this is a step down.
- **FlexJobs.** Paid subscription, and most of its listings are aggregated from sources on this list that are free.
- **Browsing Indeed or Glassdoor.** Use them for saved-search alerts only. The signal-to-noise ratio for these titles is bad enough that manual browsing is a poor use of the 90 minutes.
- **Academic postdoc boards.** Wrong track. She is staff-research, not faculty-track.

## Three résumés, not one

The lanes want different framing, and a single generic résumé will underperform in all three.

- **Lane A** leads with **Meta** and reframes the UMD role as research operations: participant pipeline, RA team management, scheduling, tooling, data quality.
- **Lane B** leads with **UMD Smith** as experimental behavioral research: design, execution, coding and analysis, plus conference presentation. Master's front and center.
- **Lane C** leads with **TRI**: eTMF, document review, regulatory compliance.

Same facts throughout, different order and emphasis. This is worth doing once, properly, before the applications start.

## Network, which outperforms all of the above

- **Smith School faculty** whose studies she runs. They place students into industry and consulting constantly and are the warmest referral source she has.
- **Meta**, where she has an alum footprint. A contractor who returns is a much easier hire than a stranger.
- **DC-AAPOR** runs local events. For someone in Reston aiming at the survey research world, showing up in person is disproportionately effective relative to the effort.

## What changes once the master's field is confirmed

The list above hedges across all three lanes because the degree is not yet known. Once it is:

- **HCI, human factors, or psychology** → Lane A becomes primary. Drop Tier 2 items 8 and 9, promote items 11 and 14.
- **Public policy, public health, or survey methodology** → Lane B becomes primary. Tier 1 item 1 and Tier 2 items 5 and 9 carry the search; deprioritize the tech aggregators entirely.
- **Marketing, business analytics, or decision science** (likely if the degree is at Smith) → Lane B plus Tier 2 item 7, and the behavioral shops in item 10 move up to weekly.
